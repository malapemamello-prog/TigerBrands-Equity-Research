# Tiger Brands Limited (JSE: TBS.JO): Equity Research Report
**Recommendation:** **BUY** | Target price **R327.60** | Current price R284.43 | Upside **+15.18%**

## Summary

An equity research report on Tiger Brands, Africa's largest listed branded FMCG manufacturer (Grains, Consumer Brands, Home & Personal Care, Exports & International). It values the company with a five-year (FY2026E–FY2030E) Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) model and compares the result with the JSE market price (May 2026).

The report covers:

- Business and ESG overview
- PESTEL and Porter's Five Forces analysis
- Five-year historical financial analysis (FY2021–FY2025)
- Macro-aligned forecasting
- DCF valuation with bear, base and bull scenarios
- Relative valuation multiples
- Key risks

**Thesis:** the FY2025 turnaround is real. Revenue grew 2.7% to R34.4bn, EBIT rose 35% to R3.8bn, and gross margin recovered from 27.7% (FY2023) to 31.3% (FY2025). The company has net cash of about R5.9bn and a debt-to-equity ratio of 2.1%. The market has not fully priced in the margin recovery, so the stock is classified **Undervalued**. The variance of +15.18% exceeds the ±5% "fairly valued" band.

## Limitations

**Modelling assumptions**

- PPI (2.30%), CPI (4.50%) and the 3.02% revenue growth are held constant for five years. Real inflation and commodity prices will vary.
- The 3.02% revenue growth is a trailing average of only four year-on-year changes (+9.9%, +9.9%, −10.4%, +2.7%), so it is a thin and volatile basis.
- Cash operating profit growth of 9.01% is a four-year historical average that runs well ahead of revenue growth. Value depends on the cost savings (R500m Continuous Improvement programme) being delivered.
- Borrowings and lease repayments are held flat, and cash is a residual, not independently forecast.
- The terminal value is about 75% of the summed present values (R18,628m of R24,844m), so the result is sensitive to WACC and terminal growth. The R245.90–R403.28 sensitivity range shows how wide the outcomes are.
- The bear case (PPI 12%) may still understate an extreme multi-season drought. Rand and commodity links are only indirectly modelled.
- Exports and African expansion are excluded from the base-case growth.

**Internal inconsistencies in the report**

- The WACC inputs do not reconcile: levered beta of 0.02, market risk premium shown as both 6.6% and 6.8%, and an after-tax cost of debt of 14.72% (higher than the cost of equity). The WACC is stated as 8.68%.
- The DCF build-up gives R323.50, while the headline target is R327.60. The report attributes the difference to a finalised model with updated inputs.
- The reported enterprise value (R46,261m) does not equal the sum of the stated PV of FCF and PV of terminal value (R24,844m). The equity bridge and per-share figure are built from the R46,261m.
- Terminal growth is 2.70% in the base case but 2.30% in the scenario table.
- CapEx is quoted as 3.16% of revenue in one place and 3.00% in another.
- Price-to-Book and Price-to-Sales are shown as percentages (for example 2.70%) instead of multiples.
- The FY2026E column is missing from the Financial Analysis sheet, which leaves a gap in the gross margin chart.
- Several sentences are truncated or garbled, for example in the dividend policy paragraph.

**Scope and data**

- Peer comparison covers only three JSE-listed peers, and peer multiples are quoted as approximate ranges.
- Inputs are secondary public data (annual reports, Stats SA, Investing.com), not independently audited. The market price is a single point in time (May 2026).
- FY2025 net profit includes non-recurring asset disposal gains, which flatter profitability and ROE.
- The share buyback reduces equity, which inflates ROE.
- Qualitative factors (governance, succession planning, private-label competition) are not quantified in the DCF.
- Analysis is a student academic exercise and not investment advice.
